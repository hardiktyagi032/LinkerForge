# Cross-domain ML mechanisms for DFL detection

Status: screened primary sources and experiment hypotheses, **not** demonstrated linker improvements. The immediate pathology is a small set of known positive spans, many masked `x` residues, and a high-precision objective that the current residue CNN does not reach with 50 true-positive residues on TE82. Its sequence-only span-head variant reaches 195 true-positive residues at 90.7% development precision but recovers only 4/46 regions at IoU 0.5. The existing TR166/TE82 screen found no exact matches or top-20 local-alignment flags at >=50% identity and >=70% one-sided coverage; it is not family-independence certification (`reports/dfl_tr166_te82_similarity_screen_2026-10-12.json`).

The most plausible **breakthrough thesis** is not a generic larger encoder. It is a *label-aware, precision-constrained span detector* that distinguishes verified negatives from unknowns, trains toward the actual high-precision endpoint, and returns coherent intervals. This is a compound hypothesis requiring separate ablations: (1) label semantics/partial annotation, (2) precision objective, (3) span structure, then combinations. None of the cited papers proves this will work in proteins or establishes novelty of the eventual combination.

Third-trial preregistration: keep the exact TR166/TE82 files and masks, frozen ESM2-35M, two-layer CNN head, seed 7, six epochs, AdamW 3e-4, batch four and APS checkpoint selection. Add a smooth precision-constraint surrogate to BCE only. Primary TE82 metric remains masked APS; secondary high-precision recall, no-positive specificity and IoU-0.5 region metrics. Compare with the existing matched CNN and span trial. The failure hypothesis is that a batchwise constraint improves confidence at the cost of APS or full-span recall. TE64 and CAID3 remain unopened for selection. One seed is screening only; no promotion or paper claim.
