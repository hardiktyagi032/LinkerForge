# Search for 10,000 unique experimentally function-tested peptide linkers

Date: 12 October 2026. This report supersedes any informal suggestion that a large *annotated* or structure-backed linker collection meets the new requirement. Inclusion now requires (1) the exact amino-acid linker sequence, (2) the proteins/modules it joined or a defined cleavage context, and (3) a measured experimental readout of linker performance, such as activity, fluorescence, cleavage, or binding. Mere genome presence, PDB deposition, predicted structure, or computational linker-boundary assignment is insufficient. Different tests of the same string are separate observations but one unique sequence. The number of *retrievable records satisfying all three* has not yet been established.

The paper describes read frequencies, mutation enrichment, and separately replicated flow-cytometry measurements. Require an explicit sequence-to-functional-readout mapping before counting a string; frequency alone is insufficient. |

Functional readout is DNA binding in native context, not general fusion performance. |

| DLD, DisProt, DisoFLAG, CAID, LinkerDB, PDB/CATH/SCOP-derived gaps | Thousands of annotation/structure records | **Exclude from the function-tested count** absent a linked wet-lab outcome for the precise sequence/context. They remain useful for separate annotation or pretraining tiers. |
| Protease substrate libraries | Some report 10^4--10^5 measured cleavage sites | Do not count generic substrate peptides unless the exact sequence was actually tested as a cleavable *linker* joining components in a defined construct. |

It is a useful assay technology for future data generation, not an existing linker dataset. |
| Marvin et al., 2011, MBP--cpGFP maltose sensors, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3265398/ | Several thousand linker-variant constructs screened for fluorescence with/without maltose | Genuine functional screening, but the main varied linkers were two residues per junction. ## Current conclusion

The 10,000-unique-sequence threshold is **not verified**. The existing 2,621-sequence LinkerForge catalog is an annotation index, not a function-tested catalog; its count must not be carried over. The immediately promising experimental publications are at the hundreds-to-low-thousands scale and may overlap or report combinations rather than distinct single linkers. Pooled sequencing, designed-library size, and per-position enrichment must not be silently converted to individual linker-function labels. A defensible 10,000 count would require a row-level cross-study audit with sequence normalization, context/outcome capture, and exact deduplication, or new experimental data generation. No model should be trained or paper claim made as though the threshold is met.
